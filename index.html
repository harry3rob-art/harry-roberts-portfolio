How my marketing got sharper, client by client

Miki and I started out making brands look professional: she designed the brands, and I wrote the copy, sales scripts and email sequences for the clients who wanted marketing too. Those early clients taught me that a professional look doesn't tell a stranger who a business is for, so Miki named that problem in her Brand Fog book and I built SnapGist, an app that scores a website the way a stranger reads it. Now every project starts with research on the buyer who decides and ends with a SnapGist score.

Beauty and wellness consultant · 2024 to 2025

The problem

After 20 years managing medical offices, the woman behind Anita B. Volpe left healthcare to help women at retirement age get their energy and their confidence back. She came to us in November 2024 with the knowledge and the warmth, and she needed a brand that would carry both to women meeting her for the first time.

What I built

Miki designed her logo, brand board and visuals, and I wrote her sales script and a marketing and copywriting guide with a six-month email calendar. From there I sent her weekly email through Kit and met with her for strategy calls through mid-2025.

What it taught meAnita B. Volpe made most of her sales in person, and the weekly emails I wrote never turned into sales because we couldn't find a reliable way to bring strangers onto her list, which taught me that a brand has to win strangers over online the way its owner wins people face to face.

Education · Sells to districts

SSG Educational

Trauma-informed teaching methodology · 2024 to 2025

The problem

SSG's founder spent more than 40 years in education, including years as a teacher and principal inside a juvenile detention school, and she sums up her method in four words: “I teach the teachers.” Teachers use her method every day, but the contract comes from school district leaders, who decide which training their teachers get.

What I built

Miki built the visual brand, and I wrote her a marketing and copywriting guide with homepage copy and welcome emails, plus an SEO keyword sheet aimed at the district leaders who buy teacher training. In March 2025 we sat down with her again for a one-hour review.

What it taught meTeachers use SSG's method every day, but district leaders decide whether to pay for it, which taught me that the person you serve and the person who signs can be different people. Copy written for the one who uses a program can miss the one who pays for it, so now I name the person who signs before I write a word, the same question that shaped my TECHSIS buyer research in 2026.

Workforce Training · Sells to workforce boards

TECHSIS

Fiber optic career training · Texas · September 2026

The problem

Deep fiber expertise and a one-week broadband bootcamp, but buyers couldn't tell whether TECHSIS was a training school, a sales company or a grant partner.

What I built

Buyer research that named the workforce development board as the real customer, a homepage built around that buyer's first question (are participants still working 90 days later?), and four pricing options benchmarked against Texas college fiber programs. A SnapGist clarity scan took the homepage from three out of 10 to eight.

A Courtney Wilson Enterprises nonprofit · Western New York · October 2026

The problem

A two-path site with families on the homepage and funders on their own giving page, plus the copy, page designs and a sponsor pitch deck. The new headline names the exact fees kids are quitting over, so a parent knows in one line whether Sincerra Kids can help.
